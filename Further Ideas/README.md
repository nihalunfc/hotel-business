# Further Ideas: Potential and Limitations

> **In short: promising ideas that are not ready yet, each listed honestly with what is missing and what would make it work.**

Ideas with real potential that are not yet ready to become full proposals. Each one has a clear limitation: data the hotel may not have, a capital cost, a dependency on another module, or a risk to guests or staff. They are listed honestly, so that the right ones can be picked up when their limitation is resolved.

| # | Idea | Potential | What it lacks or its drawbacks | What would make it viable |
| :-- | :-- | :-- | :-- | :-- |
| 1 | Water leak detection from meter data ([HARLTON-08](../Project%20HARLTON/08-water-leak-detection/proposal.md)) | One running toilet can cost about USD 2,100 a year [1] | No authoritative benchmark for the share of hotel water lost to leaks; many older buildings lack interval water meters | A low-cost meter data logger and three months of baseline data |
| 2 | Predictive maintenance for HVAC and kitchen equipment | Predictive maintenance can save 8 to 12% over preventive maintenance alone, which itself saves 12 to 18% over reactive maintenance [2] | Needs equipment sensor data (vibration, temperature, run hours) that older plants do not record | Start with run-hour and work-order history, then add sensors to the highest-cost equipment |
| 3 | Kitchen food waste measurement | Hotels in one multi-country study saved on average USD 7 for every USD 1 invested in reducing kitchen food waste, and cut waste by 21% within a year [3]; avoidable food waste in Canada is valued at about CAD 58 billion [4] | Requires weighing waste by category at every station, every day; staff discipline is the main failure point | A simple scale-and-tablet station in one outlet, with weekly results shared with the chef |
| 4 | Restaurant reservation deposits and no-show management | Operators estimate a no-show table for two costs about USD 80 on average (vendor research) [5] | Deposits can deter bookings and frustrate hotel guests who expect flexibility | Apply deposits only on compression nights and large parties; waive for in-house guests |
| 5 | Battery storage for peak shaving | Directly reduces the peak-hour charge in [HARLTON-01](../Project%20HARLTON/01-peak-hour-energy-cost/proposal.md) | High capital cost; battery packs alone were modelled at about USD 199 per kWh for a 4-hour commercial system, before installation [6]; no provincial incentive for behind-the-meter storage was found | Prove the load-shifting value first with existing equipment, then size a battery only for the remaining gap |
| 6 | Rooftop or parking-canopy solar | Provincial retrofit incentives cover up to 50% of eligible project costs [7] | Limited roof area on towers, long payback, and incentives that change year to year | Parking structures and low-rise buildings as candidates; confirm current incentive levels |
| 7 | Guest profile de-duplication and lifetime value | A vendor reports about 6% of guest profiles are duplicates, up to 16% in some cases [8]; duplicates distort repeat-guest and loyalty analysis | Vendor-reported figures only; matching guests across properties raises privacy obligations [9] | Pseudonymous matching inside the PMS, governed under the [Data Foundation](../Data%20Foundation/proposal.md) |
| 8 | Room-level attribute pricing (view, floor, corner) | Captures willingness to pay for specific rooms rather than room categories | Needs clean room-attribute data; guests may see it as unfair unless framed carefully [10] | Start with a small number of premium attributes and frame them as upgrades |
| 9 | Elevator dispatch control | Could shorten waits at check-out peaks | Elevator control is a life-safety system owned by the manufacturer; operators are unlikely to accept outside control | Influence demand instead, as in [H2-02](../Project%20H2/02-departure-flow-balancing/proposal.md) |
| 10 | RFID linen tracking | One vendor reports linen loss falling from 15-20% to 3-5% [11] | Tag cost per item, laundry-partner compatibility, and vendor-reported results | Use [HARLTON-09](../Project%20HARLTON/09-linen-and-amenity-loss-control/proposal.md) count data first to see whether loss is large enough to justify tags |
| 11 | Bulk amenity dispensers | Major chains are removing hundreds of millions of small bottles a year [12]; some jurisdictions now ban small bottles [13] | Some guests associate small bottles with luxury; dispensers need tamper-proof fittings and refill labour | Pilot in one tower with guest feedback; refill on the replenishment routes of [H2-10](../Project%20H2/10-decentralized-supply-placement/proposal.md) |
| 12 | Computer-vision room inspection | Could make inspections faster and more consistent | Cameras in guest rooms raise serious privacy concerns; likely resistance from staff and unions | Not recommended for guest rooms; limited use in back-of-house areas only, with consent |
| 13 | Group "wash" prediction | Better estimates of how much of a group block will actually be used reduce displaced revenue | Needs several years of group block and pickup history at room-night level | Collect block and pickup consistently through the [Data Foundation](../Data%20Foundation/proposal.md) |
