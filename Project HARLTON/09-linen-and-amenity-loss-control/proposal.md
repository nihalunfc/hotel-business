# HARLTON-09: Linen and Amenity Loss Control

> **In short: count linen and amenities in and out each week to find where items go missing, and fix the cause instead of just buying more.**

**Category:** Expense optimization
**Optimizes:** Replacement cost of linen, towels, robes and in-room amenities
**Status:** Proposal

---

## 1. What It Is

A simple measurement system that compares, each week, how much linen goes into circulation with how much comes back from laundry. It breaks the result down by property, tower and floor so that losses can be located instead of absorbed.

It starts with counts the hotel already takes, such as laundry batch counts, par-level counts and purchase orders. It can later scale to item-level RFID tags where the business case supports it.

## 2. Why It Matters

**Loss is high and largely unmeasured.**
- Citing the US textile-rental industry association, a 2026 trade report states that hotels "lose or discard around 15% to 20% of linen products before the end of their useful life" [1].
- The same report notes there is "no reliable global benchmark for the proportion of hotel inventory taken by guests" [1]. Most hotels do not know their own figure.

**Guests do take items.**
- In one survey, 85 percent of US and Canadian respondents admitted to having taken toiletries or towels from a hotel [2].
- One hotel that introduced tracking reported towel thefts falling "from 4,000 occurrences down to 750", saving about $16,000 a month [2].
- A linen technology provider reports losses falling from 15-20% to 3-5% with RFID tracking [3].

**Loss is a pattern, not a mystery.** Linen is lost at specific points: guest rooms, pool decks, staff areas, laundry transfers and condemned stock that is never recorded. Once counts are compared at each hand-off, the largest leak usually becomes obvious within weeks.

## 3. How It Will Be Solved

**Step 1: Count reconciliation.**
- A weekly table of items issued, returned from laundry, condemned and purchased, by item type and location.
- Unexplained loss is the difference.

**Step 2: Location breakdown.**
- Compare loss rates across properties, towers, floors and amenity areas, normalized by occupied rooms or amenity attendance.
- Highlight outliers.

**Step 3: Targeted actions.** These are tied to where the loss occurs:
- pool towel exchange tokens;
- par-level adjustments;
- condemned-stock logging;
- retail sale of robes in rooms;
- RFID tagging only for high-value items or high-loss areas.

**Step 4: Measurement.** Replacement spend per occupied room, month over month, against the prior year.

**Deliverables:**
- A weekly loss dashboard.
- An outlier list.
- A quarterly replacement-spend report.

## 4. Data Required

- Laundry batch counts, par-level counts, condemned-item logs, linen purchase orders, occupancy and amenity attendance.

## 5. Risks and Assumptions

- Count data quality varies. The first month is used to standardize counting before conclusions are drawn.
- RFID results quoted below come from a vendor and a single-hotel example. They indicate potential, not a guaranteed outcome.
