The short version: Ontario's Global Adjustment (GA) is a large charge on every electricity bill that pays for the province's power plants and contracts. Most hotels pay it per kWh (Class B). Larger users can choose Class A under the Industrial Conservation Initiative (ICI): they pay GA according to their peak demand factor, which is their share of Ontario's demand in the 5 highest-demand hours of the year (usually hot summer weekday afternoons). Cut your load in those 5 hours and next year's GA bill drops for twelve months.

3How well you'll respond

Nobody knows the peak hours in advance. Alert services flag likely peak days, so hotels act on 10 to 15 alerts a summer to be sure of catching the 5 that count.

Be honest: missing one peak costs a fifth of the year's saving. With an alert service and a written playbook, 80% (4 of 5) is a realistic target; 100% takes automation.

How it's calculated

Class A GA charge. The real charge is your peak demand factor (your demand in the peak hours ÷ Ontario's demand in those hours, averaged over the 5 hours of the May to April base period) × the total Global Adjustment for each month of the following year. This tool simplifies that to demand in the peak hours (kW) × GA cost per kW-year. The default of $450 per kW-year is a rough figure in line with recent years. It is an assumption, check with your utility: it moves with the province's total GA cost and total peak demand.

kW cut = sum over equipment of load × % you can shift, capped at your demand in the peak hours. Expected peaks caught = share caught × number of peak hours. Because the charge uses the average over the peak hours, cutting C kW in k of N hours lowers your average by C × k ÷ N. Annual saving = C × k ÷ N × GA cost per kW-year.

Pre-cooling cost = alert days × hours per alert × kW cut × rebound % × energy price. Net saving = saving minus that cost. Payback = setup cost ÷ net saving.

Eligibility. The check uses your average monthly peak (from the profile when it is switched on). Above the Class A threshold (1 MW by default) a customer can opt in; above 5 MW Class A is automatic. Between 500 kW and 1 MW, opt-in has historically been limited to certain sectors such as manufacturing and greenhouses, so a hotel in that band should check with its utility. Opting in is usually done once a year, before the base period starts.

Class B comparison = annual kWh × the Class B GA rate. Annual kWh comes from the profile, or is estimated as average peak × 8,760 hours × 55% load factor when there is no profile.

Limits. This is an estimate for planning, not a bill calculation. It ignores transmission charges that also use peak demand (which add to the saving), weather-driven changes in your load, and the risk that a missed peak lands on a day you did not get an alert.
