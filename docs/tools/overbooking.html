Overbooking Calculator

Some guests always cancel late or never show up, so a full book rarely means a full hotel. This tool tells you how many extra reservations to accept so you fill those rooms without walking too many guests, for tonight and for the next 14 nights.

How it's calculated

Show-up chance. Each booking turns into an arriving guest with probability p = (1 - cancellation rate) × (1 - no-show rate). Guests are assumed to decide independently of each other.

Arrivals. If you accept L bookings (the booking limit = rooms available C + overbooking level k), the number who arrive, S, follows a binomial distribution with L trials and chance p. The tool works out the full distribution exactly (in log space so large hotels don't overflow). Above 3,000 bookings it switches to a normal approximation with a continuity correction, which is accurate at that size.

For each level:

Expected empty rooms   = E[max(C - S, 0)]
Expected walked guests = E[max(S - C, 0)]
Chance of walking any  = P(S > C)
Value of a filled room = room rate + extras - servicing cost
Expected net value     = value of a filled room × E[min(S, C)] - walk cost × E[walked]

Recommendation. The level with the highest expected net value. With a risk cap, only levels whose chance of walking anyone is at or below the cap are considered. Levels are tested from 0 up to well past the point where extra bookings stop paying.

Assumptions and limits. The model assumes you will sell up to the booking limit (overbooking only matters when demand is strong enough). It does not model walk-ins, early departures, stay-overs that extend, or guests on multi-night stays affecting the next night; no-show fees you collect are not counted, which makes it slightly cautious. Cancellations and no-shows often run together in groups (a storm, a cancelled concert), which this model treats as independent, so on unusual nights a lower cap is wise. Rates are best taken from at least 8 weeks of history for the same day of week and booking channel.
